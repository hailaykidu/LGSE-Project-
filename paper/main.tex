\begin{abstract}
Vocabulary expansion for low-resource language adaptation requires embeddings
for tokens the pretrained model has never seen. LGSE
\citep{teklehaymanot-etal-2026-lgse} proposes initializing them from
morpheme decompositions rather than random vectors or frequency-driven
subwords, and reports that this consistently outperforms every baseline
across three tasks and two languages. We attempt to reproduce that result
with a faithful reimplementation, 150 runs over five seeds, and the original
datasets. The central claim does not reproduce: morpheme-grounded
initialization leads in 3 of 6 task--language cells rather than 6, no
difference in the matrix is separated at one standard deviation, and
off-the-shelf XLM-R---which expands no vocabulary and runs no adaptive
pretraining---leads 3 cells itself. We then identify four causes, each
measured rather than conjectured: the method is underspecified at two points
that sit on its critical path (the alignment matrix $W$ and the
regularization weight $\lambda$), only 16.7\% of new Amharic tokens are
genuinely decomposed so the mechanism is largely inert, a test-set revision
shifts every system by up to $+7.82$ accuracy points, and 1.4\% of
fine-tuning runs collapse to a degenerate all-\texttt{O} tagger whose zero
distorts any mean that includes it. Re-running both systems on all three
Amharic tasks under an equalized evaluation leaves its closest competitor
ahead on two of the three, and reverses the published direction on the task
where LGSE had appeared strongest. We release all code, data manifests,
per-seed run records and the
analysis that produced every number here.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Adapting a pretrained multilingual encoder to a morphologically rich
low-resource language usually means adding tokens to its vocabulary, which
raises the question of what vector each new token should start from. Random
initialization discards everything known about the token's form.
FOCUS \citep{dobler-de-melo-2023-focus} initializes from a weighted
combination of overlapping tokens in an auxiliary embedding space. LGSE
\citep{teklehaymanot-etal-2026-lgse} proposes instead to decompose each new
token into morphemes, embed those, and average, so that a derived form
inherits from its stem rather than from an arbitrary subword split.

The intuition is appealing for Amharic and Tigrinya, where a single surface
form carries affixes that a frequency-driven tokenizer fragments
arbitrarily.\footnote{For example, Amharic \textit{selamawi} (``peaceful'')
decomposes as \textit{selam} (``peace'') ${+}$ \textit{-awi}; LGSE's
intent is that the new token inherit from \textit{selam}.}
The published evaluation reports LGSE ahead of every baseline in
all six task--language cells, by margins of 1.3 to 9.4 points, with standard
deviations of 0.3 to 1.0 over five seeds.

This paper reports what we found when we rebuilt that pipeline. We are not
disinterested parties: we are the authors of the paper under examination, and
this work began as an attempt to make its results reproducible rather than to
refute them. We report the outcome as measured.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\itemsep0em
\item A reproduction of LGSE across 3 tasks $\times$ 2 languages $\times$ 5
  systems $\times$ 5 seeds (150 runs), with every per-seed record released.
  The central claim does not reproduce (\S\ref{sec:results}).
\item Four measured causes for the discrepancy, each isolated by a control
  rather than inferred: method underspecification, low morpheme coverage, a
  test-set confound, and degenerate runs (\S\ref{sec:why}).
\item A controlled head-to-head on all three Amharic tasks showing that when
  LGSE and its closest competitor are placed on the same evaluation, the
  competitor leads two of three and the published direction reverses on the
  third (\S\ref{sec:controlled}).
\item An argument that the reported precision ($\pm 0.3$ to $\pm 1.0$) is
  not attainable on these benchmarks at these sizes, with the measured
  seed-to-seed variance as evidence (\S\ref{sec:variance}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background}

\paragraph{LGSE.} For a new token $t$ with morpheme decomposition
$m_1 \dots m_k$, LGSE sets
\begin{equation}
e_{\text{new}} = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k} W e_{m_j},
\label{eq:lgse}
\end{equation}
where $e_{m_j}$ is a FastText vector and $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$
aligns the FastText space to the model's. Tokens with no decomposition fall
back to character $n$-grams, and during language-adaptive pretraining (LAPT)
a regularizer $L_{\text{reg}} = \lambda \lVert e_{\text{new}} - \mu \rVert^2$
discourages drift from the initialization, where $\mu$ is the initial vector.

\paragraph{What is not specified.} Equation~\ref{eq:lgse} requires $W$. The
paper states that ``a learned linear projection $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$
is applied'' and says nothing further: no training objective, no data, no
procedure, and no released artifact. Similarly, $\lambda$ ``controls the
regularization strength'' with no value, range, or sensitivity analysis
given. Both quantities sit directly on the path that distinguishes LGSE from
its baselines, so neither can be treated as an incidental detail.

We also note a structural consequence the original does not discuss. The
initializer writes $We_{m_j}$ into the embedding matrix, and $L_{\text{reg}}$
anchors to a constant $\mu$. Under the paper's stated objectives, nothing
downstream is a function of $W$ once initialization is complete, so $W$
receives no gradient during LAPT. $W$ is therefore effectively a fixed
preprocessing transform, not a learned parameter, and our substitution below
treats it as one.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Systems.} We evaluate five, matching the original: off-the-shelf
XLM-R base \citep{conneau-etal-2020-unsupervised}; \textsc{+LAPT} (adaptive
pretraining, no vocabulary expansion); \textsc{+Random+LAPT} (expansion with
randomly initialized rows); \textsc{+FOCUS+LAPT}; and \textsc{+LGSE+LAPT}.
Only the last two consume FastText vectors and therefore load $W$, which
makes \textsc{+FOCUS+LAPT} the natural control for isolating LGSE's
morpheme path: it shares every component except morpheme segmentation.

\paragraph{Data.} Amharic NER uses the official MasakhaNER splits
\citep{adelani-etal-2021-masakhaner}. Amharic QA uses AmQA
\citep{taffa-etal-2024-amqa} as released. Tigrinya QA uses an extractive
SQuAD-format conversion of TIGQA with a recorded 80/10/10 seed. Tigrinya TC
uses FineWeb-C (\texttt{tir\_Ethi}) at 597/74/74. Amharic TC uses a
2{,}499-row self-annotated educational-quality corpus (2{,}340 after
cleaning; see Appendix~\ref{app:repro}), split
1872/233/235 by the same stratified procedure as Tigrinya; the first author
led that annotation. Every split is recorded in a manifest carrying source
URL, raw checksum and split seed.

\paragraph{Substitutions.} Where the paper is silent we chose, documented,
and held fixed: $W$ is obtained by orthogonal Procrustes,
$W^\star = \arg\min_{W^\top W = I} \lVert WX - Y\rVert_F$, on surface-form
anchors shared between each language's FastText model and XLM-R (5{,}910
anchors for Amharic, 3{,}875 for Tigrinya; residual Frobenius norm 423.0
against 633.0 unaligned, and 315.1 against 490.0). We set $\lambda = 1.0$.
These are our choices, not the paper's, and results below should be read
accordingly.

\paragraph{Protocol.} Five seeds (42--46) per cell, mean $\pm$ sample
standard deviation. Accuracy for TC, F1 for NER and QA, matching the
original. Each run writes a record containing its commit, config checksum,
$W$ source, corpus checksum and per-seed dev and test metrics.

\paragraph{LGSE leads 3 of 6 cells, not 6.} It is joint-best on Amharic TC
(75.77, tied with \textsc{+LAPT} to four significant figures) and leads
Tigrinya TC and Tigrinya QA relative to \textsc{+FOCUS+LAPT}. It leads no
NER cell in either language and trails \textsc{+FOCUS+LAPT} by 3.09 on
Amharic QA.

\paragraph{Off-the-shelf XLM-R leads 3 cells} --- both NER cells and
Tigrinya QA --- while expanding no vocabulary and running no adaptive
pretraining. This is the single most awkward observation for the published
narrative, because it holds regardless of how $W$ or $\lambda$ are chosen:
the baseline that requires none of the machinery is competitive with all of
it.

\paragraph{Adaptive pretraining does not help.} \textsc{+LAPT} is below
off-the-shelf XLM-R in 5 of 6 cells. The monotonic ordering the original
reports in every column --- XLM-R $<$ \textsc{+LAPT} $<$
\textsc{+Random} $<$ \textsc{+FOCUS} $<$ \textsc{+LGSE} --- does not appear
anywhere in Table~\ref{tab:main}.

\paragraph{The gaps are not uniform.} Amharic TC is within 2.03 of the
published value while Tigrinya TC is 38.17 below it. A uniform
implementation deficit would not produce that spread; a per-cell evaluation
difference would, which motivates \S\ref{sec:why}.

\section{Why: Four Measured Causes}
\label{sec:why}

\subsection{The method is underspecified on its critical path}

$W$ and $\lambda$ are both unspecified (\S2). We substituted Procrustes and
$\lambda=1.0$ and documented both, but no reader---ourselves included, three
months later---can recover the original choices from the paper, and
Equation~\ref{eq:lgse} cannot be evaluated without them. We note the practical cost concretely: during this
work a configuration error pointed Amharic runs at the \textit{Tigrinya}
alignment matrix for several months. Because the paper specifies no $W$,
nothing in the pipeline or its outputs flagged the substitution as wrong.
Correcting it moved Amharic NER by $+2.20$ on matched seeds. A method whose
central operator can be silently replaced by the wrong language's operator,
with no detectable signal, is not specified tightly enough to reproduce.

\subsection{The mechanism is largely inert}
\label{sec:coverage}

LGSE differs from FOCUS only on tokens it can decompose. Measured against
the 198 Amharic tokens added by LAPT: 45 (22.7\%) have a lexicon entry, but
12 of those entries map a loanword to itself as a single morpheme
(\textit{television}, \textit{president}, \textit{laboratory}, and nine
more). Averaging over one element reproduces exactly the whole-token FastText
vector the next fallback tier supplies, so \textbf{genuine decomposition
covers 33 of 198 tokens (16.7\%)}. For the remaining 83.3\%, LGSE and FOCUS
differ only in which auxiliary space they draw a whole-token vector from.

This is the most important finding in the paper for anyone who wants the
method to work. LGSE's distinguishing mechanism was exercised on one token in
six; the published comparison largely measured two slightly different ways of
copying a FastText vector. Attaching HornMorpho \citep{gasser-2011-hornmorpho}
as an analyzer fallback triples genuine decomposition to 99 of 198 (50.0\%),
which is the obvious next experiment and one we have not yet run to
completion.

\subsection{A test-set revision moves every system}
\label{sec:confound}

The Amharic TC test set was rebuilt during this work, from 71 items to 235
(1.41 against 0.43 accuracy points per example). Re-running on the rebuilt
split moved \textit{every} system upward, by $+1.93$ to $+8.21$ points,
including systems that perform no vocabulary expansion at all. Any comparison
that mixes the two splits therefore measures the split as much as the
system---and we show in \S\ref{sec:controlled} that this is sufficient to
reverse a published conclusion.

\subsection{Roughly 1\% of runs collapse}
\label{sec:degenerate}

Across 429 runs, 6 (1.4\%) recorded precision, recall and F1 of exactly 0.00
on both dev and test. These are not crashes: the runs complete normally, LAPT loss
descends as usual, and total runtime is unchanged. The downstream tagger
settles into the all-\texttt{O} local minimum, proposing no entity, so both
precision and recall are genuinely zero. One such run sits inside the
\textsc{+Random+LAPT} Amharic NER cell, where averaging it in publishes
$54.22 \pm 30.44$ against $67.78 \pm 3.25$ over the four converged
seeds---a 13.5-point understatement of a baseline, and a standard deviation
that is a collapse indicator rather than a measure of seed variance. We
report such cells both ways and disclose the collapse count.

\paragraph{TC: the published direction reverses.} On the frozen 71-item split
(Table~\ref{tab:main}), LGSE leads \textsc{+FOCUS+LAPT} on this cell by
$+1.97$. On the equalized evaluation it trails by $-3.32$, and it does not
win a single seed. The control moved from 73.80 to 81.62, a shift of $+7.82$
on a system that performs no morpheme segmentation whatsoever: the rebuilt
test set, not the initializer, produced the apparent improvement, and FOCUS
captured more of it than LGSE did. We registered this prediction in the
project repository before the control finished, stating that a result near
73.80 would attribute the gain to LGSE and a result near 78 would attribute
it to the split.

\paragraph{QA: FOCUS leads, consistently.} LGSE trails by $1.17$ and is
behind on four of five seeds. The margin is small and the two standard
deviations overlap, but the sign is stable.

\paragraph{NER: LGSE leads, on three usable seeds.} This is the one task
where LGSE comes out ahead, by $+1.89$. We report it with less confidence
than the other two. Two of the ten runs in this cell collapsed to the
degenerate all-\texttt{O} tagger---LGSE on seed 45, FOCUS on seed
44---leaving three seeds on which both systems converged. A 20\% collapse
rate in a single cell, against 1.4\% across the project
(\S\ref{sec:degenerate}), means the Amharic NER fine-tune is unstable
independently of which initializer it starts from, and three paired
observations cannot separate a 1.89-point difference from that instability.

\paragraph{Overall.} FOCUS leads two of the three Amharic tasks on matched
data, LGSE leads the third, and no difference is separated at one standard
deviation. Taken with Table~\ref{tab:main}, the picture is consistent:
morpheme-grounded initialization is not distinguishable from
frequency-driven initialization on these benchmarks, and where it appeared to
be, the appearance came from an evaluation difference rather than from the
method. The three remaining Amharic baselines (XLM-R, \textsc{+LAPT},
\textsc{+Random+LAPT}) have not been re-run on the rebuilt splits, so the
full column still cannot be ranked; what can be compared is the pair that the
paper's claim rests on.

majority class.

We therefore regard $\pm 0.3$ on a 200-item QA test set over five seeds as
not attainable, and the published precision as evidence that the original
variance was computed over something other than five independent
fine-tuning runs.

\section{Arithmetic Errors in the Published Table}

Of the 15 \textit{Avg} values in the published Table 2, two are not the mean
of the two language columns they summarize: \textsc{+LAPT} TC prints 67.8
where $(69.4+71.0)/2 = 70.20$, and \textsc{+LAPT} NER prints 70.4 where
$(69.8+75.0)/2 = 72.40$. The remaining 13 are correct. Both errors
understate the \textsc{+LAPT} baseline, which is one of LGSE's comparison
points.

\section{What Would Make This Reproducible}
\label{sec:recs}

The causes in \S\ref{sec:why} generalize beyond this paper, and each has a
cheap remedy that we would have benefited from.

\paragraph{Release the operator, not just the equation.} $W$ is a $768
\times 768$ matrix, 2.3\,MB. Releasing it, or the twenty lines that build it,
would have removed the single largest obstacle to reproducing LGSE. Where a
method introduces a learned or fitted transform, the artifact is part of the
method. We release ours with a sidecar recording its language, anchor count,
objective and residual Frobenius norm, so that a reader can tell at a glance
whether they have the right matrix---which, as \S\ref{sec:why} describes, we
once did not.

\paragraph{Report the coverage of the mechanism.} LGSE differs from its
baselines only on tokens it can decompose. One number---the fraction of new
tokens that actually receive a multi-morpheme decomposition---would have
revealed that the published comparison exercised the mechanism on 16.7\% of
its vocabulary. We suggest that any paper proposing a conditional
initialization strategy report the rate at which its condition fires. It is a
single line and it bounds the effect size the method can possibly have.

\paragraph{Run the system that shares everything but the contribution.}
\textsc{+FOCUS+LAPT} and \textsc{+LGSE+LAPT} differ only in morpheme
segmentation, so when the test set changed, their joint movement separated the
evaluation change from the method
(\S\ref{sec:controlled}). Reporting absolute numbers
alone cannot do this. We found three confounds this way and would have found
none without it.

\paragraph{Disclose degenerate runs rather than averaging them.} A single
all-\texttt{O} seed moved a baseline by 13.5 points and inflated its standard
deviation to 30.44 (\S\ref{sec:degenerate}). Neither dropping such runs
silently nor averaging them silently is adequate; we report both means and
the collapse count, and let the reader decide.

\paragraph{Check that the between-system spread exceeds the between-seed
spread.} In three of six cells here it does not (\S\ref{sec:variance}), which
means the experiment could not have supported a ranking no matter which system
won. This is a precondition for a comparison, not a post-hoc caveat, and it is
computable before any system is proposed.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Embedding initialization for vocabulary expansion.} Adapting a
multilingual encoder to a new language by extending its vocabulary requires a
vector for every added token, and the choice of initializer is the subject of
a small literature. WECHSEL \citep{minixhofer-etal-2022-wechsel} transfers
embeddings through bilingual dictionaries and static word vectors. FOCUS
\citep{dobler-de-melo-2023-focus}, our principal baseline, initializes each
new token as a sparse weighted combination of the tokens it overlaps with in
an auxiliary FastText space, and is the closest prior method to LGSE: both
project from FastText into the model's space, and both reduce to a
whole-token copy when their decomposition or overlap is unavailable. That
shared fallback is precisely why the coverage measurement in
\S\ref{sec:coverage} matters, and why we chose FOCUS as the control in
\S\ref{sec:controlled}. \citet{csaki-etal-2023-efficiently} survey the
broader adaptation pipeline. Our results do not speak against this line of
work; they indicate that on these benchmarks, at these sizes, the choice among
initializers is not resolvable with five seeds.

\paragraph{Morphological segmentation for Ethio-Semitic languages.}
Amharic and Tigrinya are templatic and heavily affixing, which motivates
morpheme-aware vocabulary construction in the first place. HornMorpho
\citep{gasser-2011-hornmorpho} provides finite-state analysis for both, and
is what we attach in \S\ref{sec:coverage} to raise genuine decomposition from
16.7\% to 50.0\%. The gap between a static lexicon and an analyzer is the
practical difference between a mechanism that fires on one token in six and
one that fires on one in two.

\paragraph{Reproduction studies.} Our methodology follows the control-based
approach advocated in the reproducibility literature
\citep{belz-etal-2021-systematic}: rather than reporting only that numbers
differ, we hold systems that should be unaffected by a change fixed and use
their movement to attribute the difference. Reproductions of this kind are
usually conducted by third parties; this one is a self-examination, with the
conflict of interest and its mitigations stated in the Ethics Statement.

\section{Conclusion}

LGSE's published claim of consistent improvement over all baselines does not
reproduce. Under a faithful reimplementation it leads 3 of 6 cells, no
difference is statistically separated, off-the-shelf XLM-R leads 3 cells
itself, and when we equalize the evaluation across all three Amharic tasks
its closest competitor leads two of them.

We do not conclude that morpheme-grounded initialization is a bad idea. We
conclude that the published evaluation did not test it: with genuine
decomposition reaching 16.7\% of new tokens, LGSE and FOCUS were largely the
same system in five of six cells. The informative experiment---raising
coverage to 50\% with a morphological analyzer and re-running with controls
on a common split---is specified in \S\ref{sec:coverage} and remains open. We
release everything needed to run it.

\section*{Limitations}

Our $W$ and $\lambda$ are substitutions, not the originals, so our absolute
numbers are not strictly comparable to the published ones and a reader should
treat the per-cell gaps in Table~\ref{tab:main} as the gap to \textit{a}
faithful reimplementation rather than \textit{the} one. The controlled
comparison in \S\ref{sec:controlled} covers two systems on three Amharic
tasks; the other three Amharic baselines on the rebuilt splits, and all
Tigrinya cells under the same control, remain to be run. Its NER cell rests
on three paired seeds after two degenerate runs were excluded, which is too
few to support a directional claim on that task. The HornMorpho coverage result is a measurement of
segmentation reach, not of downstream accuracy: we have not completed runs at
50\% coverage, so we make no claim about what LGSE achieves there. Tigrinya
QA is not comparable to the official TIGQA benchmark (single-reference
answers against the official three or more, our splits against the official
ones, and the published benchmark figure uses XLM-R large where we use base).
The two TC columns are not comparable to each other: Amharic's majority-class
baseline is 66.4\% against Tigrinya's 32.4\%, so any average over them mixes
incommensurable scales. Finally, five seeds is what the original used and what
we matched; \S\ref{sec:variance} shows it is too few for these benchmarks, and
a definitive comparison would need more.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

This paper examines a published work by the same authors. We report the
reproduction outcome as measured, including where it contradicts our own
earlier claims, and we document errors introduced during this reproduction
alongside those found in the original: a configuration error that applied the
Tigrinya alignment matrix to Amharic runs (\S\ref{sec:why}), and a coverage
figure initially over-reported as 22.7\% before re-measurement established
16.7\% (\S\ref{sec:coverage}). We found no evidence of fabrication in the
original work; every discrepancy we traced is consistent with ordinary
pipeline defects of the kind we also made ourselves here.

Self-examination carries an obvious conflict of interest. We have tried to
mitigate it by pre-registering the one controlled comparison before it ran
(\S\ref{sec:controlled}), by releasing every per-seed record so that each
number in this paper can be recomputed independently, and by reporting the
outcome that is least favourable to our own prior claim. Readers should
nonetheless weigh our conclusions accordingly.

All datasets are publicly available or released by us, and the Amharic
educational-quality annotation was led by the first author. No human subjects
were involved.

\section{Reproduction Details}
\label{app:repro}

\paragraph{Code and artifacts.} All code, data manifests, per-seed run
records and the analysis scripts that produced every number in this paper are
at \url{https://github.com/hailaykidu/LGSE-Project-}. Each run writes an
\texttt{experiment.json} recording the commit, config SHA-256, alignment
matrix path, LAPT corpus checksum, FastText manifest and per-seed dev and
test metrics, so any figure in Tables~\ref{tab:main}--\ref{tab:snr} can be
recomputed from the records rather than taken on trust.

\paragraph{Hyperparameters.} LAPT: frozen backbone, maximum sequence length
256, MLM probability 0.15, learning rate $5\times10^{-5}$ with a constant
schedule, batch size 32, 10 epochs, weight decay 0.01, AdamW
($\epsilon = 10^{-8}$, $\beta = (0.9, 0.999)$), fp16. LGSE: character
$n$-gram range 3--5, $\lambda = 1.0$, FastText dimension 768. Downstream
fine-tuning: learning rate $5\times10^{-5}$, batch size 32, 10 epochs. These
follow the published Table 1 where it specifies a value and are recorded in
\texttt{configs/} where it does not.

\paragraph{Corpora and models.} LAPT corpora are 204{,}137 lines (1.98M
words) for Amharic and 200{,}000 lines (1.53M words) for Tigrinya. FastText
models are trained with skipgram, dimension 768, 5 epochs, minimum count 5,
giving vocabularies of 165{,}471 (Amharic) and 156{,}687 (Tigrinya).
Vocabulary expansion adds 198 Amharic and 200 Tigrinya tokens, selected by
XLM-R subword fragmentation rate over the task corpora.

\paragraph{Splits.} Amharic NER uses official MasakhaNER splits. Amharic QA
uses AmQA as released. Tigrinya TC is FineWeb-C \texttt{tir\_Ethi} at
597/74/74. Tigrinya QA is an extractive conversion of TIGQA with a recorded
80/10/10 seed. Amharic TC is 1872/233/235 from a 2{,}499-row annotated corpus
(2{,}340 after removing 2 spreadsheet-error rows and 157 exact duplicates),
stratified with seed 42 and verified leakage-free.

\paragraph{Compute.} Each (system, task, seed) unit takes approximately 5
hours on one NVIDIA A100, dominated by the 10 LAPT epochs, which are repeated
per seed because the seed determines the initialization under test. The 150
runs in Table~\ref{tab:main} therefore represent roughly 750 GPU-hours.
