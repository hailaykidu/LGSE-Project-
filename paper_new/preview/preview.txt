Abstract

Extending a pretrained encoder’s vocabulary to a new language requires an embedding for every
added token, and several initialization strategies have been proposed to supply one. We ask whether the
benchmarks commonly used for Amharic can distinguish between them. Under a single fixed protocol—
one configuration, one set of rebuilt data splits, one untuned fine-tuning recipe, shared seeds—we com-
pare morpheme-grounded initialization, overlap-based initialization, and no vocabulary extension at all,
across text classification, named entity recognition and extractive question answering. Across six paired
comparisons, every 95% confidence interval contains zero. On all three tasks the seed-to-seed variation
exceeds the entire spread between the three systems, and on text classification the smallest difference five
seeds could detect at conventional power is 19.8 macro-F1 points against an observed between-system
spread of 9.0. A further property of the setup bounds what any such comparison can show: 6 of 60
runs never fit their training data and settle into degenerate predictors, distributed across all three systems
and identifiable from training loss alone. We also record that a favourable comparison reported under a
superseded protocol reverses under this one, and that the two protocols differ in test set, training set and
class prior together, so the shift cannot be attributed to any single cause. We report means and intervals
and claim no ordering. This paper replaces a retracted predecessor [Teklehaymanot et al., 2026]; all
code, data manifests, per-run records and the script that generates every number are released.

1 Introduction

A multilingual encoder adapted to a new language by vocabulary extension needs a vector for each token
it has never seen. Random initialization discards what is known about the token’s form. FOCUS [Dobler
and de Melo, 2023] builds each new embedding from tokens it overlaps with in an auxiliary space. A
morpheme-grounded alternative decomposes the token and averages over its morphemes, which is appealing
for Amharic, where affixation is heavy and a frequency-driven tokenizer fragments surface forms arbitrarily.
Papers in this area report differences of one to several points between such strategies, with standard
deviations below one point, and conclude in favour of one of them. This paper asks a prior question: at the
sample sizes conventionally used, can these benchmarks support such a conclusion at all?

We answer it for Amharic by running three systems under one protocol and examining the variance
structure rather than only the means. The answer is that they cannot. We also measure a fine-tuning instabil-
ity that costs 10% of runs, and we document a favourable comparison reported under a superseded protocol
that reverses under this one.

2 Systems

XLM-R. Off-the-shelf XLM-R base [Conneau et al., 2020], fine-tuned directly on each task. No vocab-
ulary extension, no adaptive pretraining, no auxiliary embedding space. This is the far control: it bounds
what the rest of the machinery is worth.

FOCUS. Vocabulary extension with FOCUS initialization [Dobler and de Melo, 2023], followed by language-
adaptive pretraining. Each new embedding is a weighted combination of overlapping tokens in a FastText
space, projected into the model’s space. This is the near control: it consumes the same auxiliary space and
the same projection as the morpheme-grounded system, and differs only in not using morpheme segmenta-
tion.

3 Setup

One protocol. All 60 runs share a single configuration file, verified by checksum: every run records the
same config_sha256. Fine-tuning uses a constant learning rate of 5 × 10−5, no warmup, batch size 32,
10 epochs, and checkpoint selection by evaluation loss. This recipe is identical for all three systems and was
not tuned for any of them; the selection metric is task-agnostic by construction. Appendix A records the
checksum and the full recipe.

Data. For NER we take the MasakhaNER splits [Adelani et al., 2021] unchanged, and for question an-
swering the AmQA splits [Taffa et al., 2024] unchanged. Text classification uses a 2,499-row annotated
educational-quality corpus, reduced to 2,340 after removing 2 spreadsheet-error rows and 157 exact dupli-
cates, split 1872/233/235 by a stratified procedure at seed 42 and verified leakage-free. The first author led
that annotation. Each split carries a manifest with source, raw checksum and split seed.

Metrics. F1 for NER and QA. For text classification we report macro-F1 over the five classes present in
the test split as the primary metric, with accuracy secondary. The majority class holds 66.4% of the test set,
so accuracy is dominated by it: the majority-class baseline is 66.38 accuracy against 15.96 macro-F1. We
also report the macro-F1 value recorded in the run files, which averages over six classes including one with
no test instances and is therefore capped at five sixths of the attainable maximum.

Seeds. 42–46 for every cell, extended to 42–51 on NER for all three systems after the failure mode of §7
appeared there.

Scope. Amharic only, three systems. Two further systems from the retracted design—adaptive pretraining
alone, and extension with random initialization—were not run under this protocol, and §10 explains why
results from an earlier batch cannot substitute for them.

5 Why the Comparison Does Not Resolve

Table 3 compares the spread between the three systems against the spread between seeds of one system. On
every task the second exceeds the first. Excluding the failed runs of §7 does not change the conclusion: the
ratios become 0.56, 0.64 and 0.77.

The same point in units of detectable effect: using the observed paired-difference standard deviations,
the minimum difference detectable at α = 0.05 and 80% power is 19.80 macro-F1 on text classification,
3.10 F1 on NER and 2.32 F1 on QA. The text-classification figure is more than twice the entire observed
spread between systems. Reaching a 2-point detectable difference there would require approximately 281
seeds per system. We report this as a property of the benchmark and the sample size, not of any system.

6 A Comparison That Did Not Survive Re-measurement

An earlier batch of runs, under a superseded protocol, reported the morpheme-grounded system ahead of
FOCUS on text classification by 1.97 accuracy points. Under the protocol of §3 it trails by 3.32 and wins
no seed. We record this because the direction of a published comparison reversed, and because the reason is
instructive about comparing across protocol revisions.

The two protocols are not two measurements of the same task.
It would be convenient to read FOCUS’s
movement from 73.80 to 81.62 as the effect of a larger test set on a system that performs no morpheme
segmentation. That reading is not available. Rebuilding the corpus changed the training split and the label
distribution as well as the test split: the majority class holds 35.21% of the superseded test set and 66.38%
of the current one, so a classifier that predicts only the majority label gains 31.17 accuracy points from
the change alone, with no change in capability. Accuracy on the two splits is scaled differently, and no
per-system difference between the columns can be attributed to a single cause.

What the reversal does establish. Within the current protocol, measured on one split with one configura-
tion and shared seeds, the morpheme-grounded system does not lead FOCUS on text classification; it trails
by 3.32 points and loses every seed. That comparison is internally valid and is the one reported in Table 1.
The earlier favourable comparison is superseded, not contradicted by a controlled intervention, and we do
not claim to have isolated why it differed.

Consequence for the missing systems. Two systems from the original design were not run under the
current protocol (§10). Their numbers exist only in the superseded batch, on the 71-item split with a 35.21%
majority class. Placing those values in Table 1 beside systems measured against a 66.38% majority class
would produce an ordering driven by the class prior rather than by the systems. We therefore leave those
rows empty rather than fill them across protocols.

7 A Fine-Tuning Failure Mode

Six of 60 runs never fit their training data. Their relative training-loss reduction from the first to the last
logged epoch falls between 21.7% and 28.9%, against 69.5% to 97.9% for every other run—a gap with no
runs inside it. Any threshold between 28.9% and 69.5% selects the same six, so the classification does not
depend on where in the gap it is placed. The rule uses training loss only and never a test score.

The resulting predictors are degenerate in a task-specific way. On NER the tagger proposes no entity at
all, giving precision, recall and F1 of exactly zero. On text classification the classifier never predicts class
3, which holds 19% of the test set, and never predicts the two rarest classes; per-class F1 shows this as a
partial label collapse rather than a total one. Dev scores track test scores in each case, so the failure is visible
without touching the test set.

These runs are not checkpoint mis-selection: the evaluation-loss minimum across all ten saved check-
points is 0.77–0.80 in the failed runs against 0.42–0.55 in the successful ones, so no available checkpoint
was good. Nor is it overfitting: training loss stays high.

We retain all six runs in Table 1 and report a secondary analysis excluding them. Excluding them moves
the text-classification means to 54.67, 54.56 and 56.45 for LGSE, FOCUS and XLM-R, on three, five and
three runs, and the NER means to 68.27, 66.78 and 67.05 on nine, nine and ten runs. Neither policy separates
the systems: the intervals remain wide and overlapping under both. We report both because the choice moves
the text-classification means by nine points and the NER means by seven, and we see no principled basis for
preferring one.

8 Recommendations

Each observation above has a cheap remedy that we would have benefited from.

Check resolution before comparing. Whether the between-system spread exceeds the between-seed
spread is computable from a pilot, before any method is proposed. Where it does not, as in three of three
tasks here, a five-seed comparison cannot support a ranking regardless of which system wins.

Report the minimum detectable difference. One line per comparison. It distinguishes “no difference
was found” from “no difference of this size could have been found”.

Run a system that shares everything but the contribution. FOCUS differs from LGSE only in mor-
pheme segmentation, so the pair bounds what the segmentation itself can be worth. A system compared
only against an unmodified baseline cannot separate the contribution from everything else that changed
alongside it.

Do not compare across protocol revisions. When a corpus is rebuilt, the test set, the training set and the
class prior can all move together, as they did here (§6): the majority-class baseline went from 35.21% to
66.38%, which alone rescales accuracy by 31 points. Numbers from before and after such a change are not
two measurements of one quantity, and a table that mixes them reports the revision rather than the systems.
Re-run the baselines or leave the cells empty.

6

Release the operator, not only the equation. W in Equation 1 is 2.3 MB. Releasing it, or the code that
builds it, removes the largest obstacle to reproducing a method of this kind. We release ours with a sidecar
recording its language, anchor count, objective and residual.

Disclose degenerate runs. A single all-O seed can move a mean by 13 points and inflate a standard
deviation past 30. Neither silent exclusion nor silent averaging is adequate; report both and state the rule.

9 Related Work

Initialization for vocabulary extension. WECHSEL [Minixhofer et al., 2022] transfers embeddings through
bilingual dictionaries and static vectors. FOCUS [Dobler and de Melo, 2023], our near control, builds each
new embedding from overlapping tokens in an auxiliary FastText space. Both FOCUS and the morpheme-
grounded method reduce to a whole-token vector when their respective condition—overlap, or a decomposition—
is unavailable, which is why they are the right pair to compare and why their difference is bounded by how
often that condition fires. Csaki et al. [2023] survey the adaptation pipeline. Our results do not argue against
this line of work; they indicate that these benchmarks at these sample sizes cannot adjudicate between its
members.

Morphological resources for Ethio-Semitic languages. Amharic is templatic and heavily affixing, which
motivates morpheme-aware vocabulary construction. HornMorpho [Gasser, 2011] provides finite-state anal-
ysis for Amharic and Tigrinya and is enabled in the configuration used here; with it, 99 of the 198 added
Amharic tokens (50.0%) receive a decomposition into more than one morpheme, against 33 (16.7%) from
the static lexicon alone. We report these as properties of the pipeline and do not use them to explain the
comparison outcomes.

Reproduction and statistical practice. Belz et al. [2021] survey reproducibility in NLP. Our methodol-
ogy is control-based: rather than reporting that numbers differ, we hold systems that should be unaffected
by a change fixed and attribute the difference from their movement.

10 Conclusion

Under one protocol, on three Amharic tasks, no paired comparison between morpheme-grounded initial-
ization, overlap-based initialization and no extension at all excludes zero. The reason is measurable and
specific: changing the seed moves a system further than changing the system does, the smallest detectable
difference on text classification is twice the observed spread, and a tenth of runs fail to fit their training data.
A comparison reported under a superseded protocol reverses under this one.

We do not conclude that these initializers are equivalent; our tests are too small to support that, and we
say so. We conclude that this evaluation suite, at the sample sizes the field conventionally uses, does not
answer the question it is being asked, and that the four properties above are worth measuring before the next
comparison of this kind is published.

Limitations

Our W and λ are documented substitutions, not values recovered from the original method, so the LGSE
column is one faithful instantiation and the absolute numbers would differ under another.

7

Two systems from the original design—adaptive pretraining alone, and extension with random initialization—

were not run under this protocol. An earlier batch contains them, but evaluated text classification on the
superseded 71-item test set, carries three different configuration checksums, and mixes two alignment ma-
trices; §6 shows that placing those numbers beside these would create an ordering artifact of about 8 points.
We therefore leave the rows empty rather than fill them from a different protocol. Completing them under
this protocol would add two rows at five seeds each, which Table 3 and the detectable-difference figures
indicate would not change the conclusion.

The paired tests have five to ten observations. They establish that these data do not separate the sys-
tems, not that the systems are equivalent; distinguishing those readings would need many more seeds or
benchmarks with a larger between-system spread.

This study covers Amharic. No cell for a second language exists under this protocol, so we make no

cross-linguistic claim.

The text-classification corpus is small, its label distribution is heavily skewed, and one of its six la-
bels has no test instances. Our primary metric excludes that label; the recorded six-class value is reported
alongside for traceability.

Ethics Statement

This paper replaces a retracted paper by the same authors. We report the outcome as measured, including
where it contradicts our own earlier claims, and we document errors made during this work alongside those
found in the original: a configuration error that applied the Tigrinya alignment matrix to Amharic runs,
a morpheme-coverage figure initially over-reported before re-measurement, and three log-parsing defects
in our own analysis code, each of which produced a wrong failure classification and is now pinned by a
regression test. We found no evidence of fabrication in the original work; every discrepancy we traced is
consistent with ordinary pipeline defects of the kind we also made here.

Self-examination carries a conflict of interest. We mitigate it by pre-registering the one directional
control before it ran (§6), by releasing every per-run record so that each number can be recomputed inde-
pendently, and by reporting the outcome least favourable to our own prior claim. Readers should weigh our
conclusions accordingly.

All datasets are publicly available or released by us. The Amharic educational-quality annotation was

led by the first author. No human subjects were involved.
