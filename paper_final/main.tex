\begin{abstract}
Extending a pretrained encoder's vocabulary to a new language requires an
embedding for every added token, and several strategies have been proposed to
supply one. We measure five of them under a single protocol per language --
one configuration, one set of data splits, one untuned fine-tuning recipe,
shared seeds -- across text classification, named entity recognition and
extractive question answering, in Amharic and Tigrinya: 165 runs filling all
thirty cells of the comparison. Morpheme-grounded initialization has the
highest mean in 1 of 6 task--language cells. Off-the-shelf XLM-R, which
expands no vocabulary and performs no adaptive pretraining at a twentieth of
the compute, leads 3. Of 24 paired comparisons against the four baselines,
morpheme-grounded initialization is ahead in 7, and no comparison against
random Gaussian initialization separates in either direction on any task or
language. We also report two properties of the setup that bound what such a
comparison can show: seed variation is comparable to the entire spread
between the five systems, and 6 of 165 runs settle into degenerate predictors
identifiable from training loss alone. This paper replaces a retracted
predecessor \citep{teklehaymanot-etal-2026-lgse}; all code, data manifests,
per-run records and the script that generates every number are released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A multilingual encoder adapted to a new language by vocabulary extension
needs a vector for every token it has not seen. The choice of initializer is
the subject of a small literature. Random initialization discards what is
known about the token's form. FOCUS \citep{dobler-de-melo-2023-focus} builds
each new embedding from tokens it overlaps with in an auxiliary space. A
morpheme-grounded alternative decomposes the token and averages over its
morphemes, which is appealing for Amharic and Tigrinya, where affixation is
heavy and a frequency-driven tokenizer fragments surface forms arbitrarily.

Papers in this area report differences of one to several points between such
strategies, with standard deviations below one point, and conclude in favour
of one of them. We measure the comparison directly, under one protocol per
language, with all five systems and both languages present.

The result is negative and specific. Morpheme-grounded initialization leads
one cell of six. It is not separated from random Gaussian initialization
anywhere. And the variance structure explains why a five-seed comparison on
these benchmarks cannot resolve the question regardless of which systems are
in it.

\paragraph{Relation to a retracted paper.} An earlier paper by these authors
described the morpheme-grounded method and reported a results table for which
no archived execution record exists, a projection matrix that was never
specified, and a dataset that was not constructed. That paper
\citep{teklehaymanot-etal-2026-lgse} is retracted and this paper replaces it.
This paper shares no text and no results with it. All data splits were
rebuilt from their original sources and are documented in
\S\ref{sec:setup} and Appendix~\ref{app:prov}, which maps every number
reported here to the job identifier, seed and result file it came from.

A first attempt at this measurement, run in September 2026, is also
withdrawn, for two reasons found by auditing our own records: every Amharic
run of the morpheme-grounded system loaded the \emph{Tigrinya} alignment
matrix, and its Tigrinya question-answering used a test split that has since
been replaced. Neither defect was visible from the tables. The runs reported
here are a later, separately configured measurement; \S\ref{sec:setup} states
the protocol each cell belongs to.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\itemsep0em
\item A complete five-system comparison on two languages and three tasks ---
  thirty cells, 165 runs, one protocol per language --- in which
  morpheme-grounded initialization leads one cell
  (\S\ref{sec:results}).
\item The floor comparison the published evaluation omitted: against random
  Gaussian initialization, the morpheme-grounded method is not separated on
  any task in either language (\S\ref{sec:floor}).
\item A measurement of the benchmarks' resolution: seed variation is

\item A fine-tuning failure mode affecting 6 of 165 runs, separable from the
  method under test using training loss alone (\S\ref{sec:failures}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Systems}

All five systems share one backbone, XLM-R base
\citep{conneau-etal-2020-unsupervised}, and differ only in whether the
vocabulary is extended and how the new rows are initialized.

\paragraph{XLM-R.} Fine-tuned directly. No vocabulary extension, no adaptive
pretraining, no auxiliary space. The far control: it bounds what the rest of
the machinery is worth.

\paragraph{+LAPT.} Vocabulary extended, new rows left at the model's default
initialization, then language-adaptive pretraining. Isolates the
contribution of adaptive pretraining itself.

\paragraph{+Random+LAPT.} New rows drawn from a Gaussian at the pretrained
embedding scale. The floor: any initializer that does not beat this is not
using the information it claims to use.

\paragraph{+FOCUS+LAPT.} FOCUS initialization
\citep{dobler-de-melo-2023-focus}: each new embedding a weighted combination
of overlapping tokens in a FastText space, projected into the model's space.
The near control --- it consumes the same auxiliary space and the same
projection as the morpheme-grounded system and differs only in not using
morpheme segmentation.

\paragraph{+LGSE+LAPT.} The morpheme-grounded initializer. A new token $t$
with decomposition $m_1 \dots m_k$ receives
\begin{equation}
e_{\text{new}} = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k} W e_{m_j},
\label{eq:lgse}
\end{equation}
with $e_{m_j}$ a FastText vector and $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ aligning
the two spaces. Tokens without a decomposition fall back to a whole-token
vector and then to character $n$-grams. During adaptive pretraining a term
$\lambda\lVert e_{\text{new}} - \mu\rVert^2$ penalizes drift from the
initialization.

\paragraph{Two quantities the method does not fix.}
Equation~\ref{eq:lgse} requires $W$ and the regularizer requires $\lambda$.
Neither is determined by the method as described, and the retracted paper
specified neither. We chose both, documented the choice, and held it fixed:
$W$ by orthogonal Procrustes on surface anchors shared between each
language's FastText model and XLM-R (5{,}910 anchors for Amharic, 3{,}875 for
Tigrinya), and $\lambda = 1.0$. These are our choices; the
\textsc{+LGSE+LAPT} column is one faithful instantiation rather than the only
one.

We note a structural consequence. The initializer writes $We_{m_j}$ into the
embedding matrix and the regularizer anchors to a constant, so nothing
downstream is a function of $W$ once initialization is complete. $W$ is a
fixed preprocessing transform here, not a learned parameter --- and because
$W$ is read only on the path that consumes FastText, \textsc{+LAPT} and
\textsc{+Random+LAPT} never load it at all. That fact is used in
Appendix~\ref{app:prov} and is verified there, not assumed.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{One protocol per language.} Every Amharic run shares one
configuration file and every Tigrinya run shares another, each verified by
checksum. The two differ only in what the language requires --- its
alignment matrix, its adaptive-pretraining corpus, its task data --- and in
one asymmetry stated below. Every hyperparameter is identical between them:
fine-tuning uses a constant learning rate of $5\times10^{-5}$, no warmup,
batch size 32, 10 epochs, and checkpoint selection by evaluation loss. This
recipe is the same for all five systems and was not tuned for any of them;
the selection metric is task-agnostic by construction.

\paragraph{The one asymmetry.} The Amharic configuration enables the amseg
morphological analyzer, which exists for Amharic and not for Tigrinya. The
morpheme-grounded system therefore decomposes 99 of 198 added Amharic tokens
(50.0\%) against 33 (16.7\%) from the static lexicon alone, while Tigrinya
has the lexicon only. We report this as a property of the available
resources. It favours the method on Amharic, which is where it leads a cell.

\paragraph{Data.} Amharic NER uses the MasakhaNER splits
\citep{adelani-etal-2021-masakhaner} unchanged and Amharic QA the AmQA splits
\citep{taffa-etal-2024-amqa} unchanged. Tigrinya NER uses MasakhaNER
likewise. Tigrinya QA is an extractive conversion of TIGQA keeping only spans
that resolve verbatim in their context: 1{,}517 of 2{,}179 pairs, split
1189/177/151 at seed 42. Amharic text classification uses a 2{,}499-row
annotated educational-quality corpus, 2{,}340 after removing 2
spreadsheet-error rows and 157 exact duplicates, split 1872/233/235. Tigrinya
text classification uses FineWeb-C \texttt{tir\_Ethi} at 597/74/74. Each
split carries a manifest with source, checksum and split seed. The first
author led the Amharic annotation.

\paragraph{The text-classification label set.} The upstream annotation
schemes record an educational-value level by name (None, Minimal, Basic,
Good, Excellent) alongside a separate problematic-content flag. For
classification we encode both in one integer column, 1--5 for the quality
levels and 6 for the flag; that encoding is ours, and it is what the task
runner sees, as six unordered classes. The flag is not a sixth point above
Excellent, so it is not on the same dimension as the rest, which is why the
primary metric below excludes it. Which quality levels occur in a given test
split is a sampling matter and differs between the two languages: the
Amharic test split contains no Excellent documents and the Tigrinya one
contains a single instance.

\paragraph{Metrics.} F1 for NER and QA. For text classification we report
macro-F1 over the label values with at least one test instance as primary,
read per run from the per-class supports, with accuracy secondary. A class
with no test instances contributes zero to a macro average without measuring
anything, so including it would depress one language's scores relative to the
other by construction. Accuracy is secondary because of the class prior:
Amharic's majority quality level holds 66.95\% of its quality-rated test
items and Tigrinya's 32.88\%, so accuracy on the two is differently scaled
and largely measures majority-level agreement on Amharic. The majority
baselines are 66.38 accuracy and 20.05 quality macro-F1 for Amharic, 32.43
and 9.90 for Tigrinya.

\paragraph{Seeds.} 42--46 for every cell, extended to 42--51 on Amharic NER
for the three systems available when the failure mode of
\S\ref{sec:failures} appeared there.

\paragraph{Across languages.} The two language columns are not two
measurements of one quantity. Their text-classification tasks have different
class priors and different test-set sizes (235 against 74 items), so the
columns must not be pooled or averaged. All comparisons we report are within
a language.

system sits

with the widest Amharic spread, XLM-R and \textsc{+LGSE+LAPT}, are also the

The comparison that matters most for a method claiming to use morphological
information is against an initializer that uses none. The published
evaluation of the morpheme-grounded method reported this baseline; the
measurement in Table~\ref{tab:floor} is, to our knowledge, the first on a
common protocol.

The result is the same in all six cells: no separation. The differences range
from $-8.71$ to $+5.02$ and every interval spans zero. Four favour random
initialization, two favour the morpheme-grounded one.

This is not a claim that the two are equivalent --- \S\ref{sec:resolution}
shows these data cannot support that either. It is the narrower and firmer
claim that on these benchmarks, at these sample sizes, decomposing a token
into morphemes and averaging pretrained vectors over them yields no
detectable advantage over drawing the same embeddings from a Gaussian.

Two further properties of the design limit what any $p$-value here can mean.
First, of the 24 paired comparisons in Table~\ref{tab:paired}, ten have an
effective $n$ of five or fewer, where the smallest attainable two-sided exact
Wilcoxon $p$ is 0.062 at $n=5$ and 0.125 at $n=4$: significance at the
conventional threshold is unreachable by construction, and a $p$-value that
cannot fall below the threshold is uninformative about the hypothesis.
Second, the minimum difference detectable at $\alpha = 0.05$ and 80\% power,
computed from the observed paired standard deviations, is well above the
differences under discussion on text classification in both languages.

\section{A Fine-Tuning Failure Mode}
\label{sec:failures}

Six of the 165 runs never fit their training data. Their relative training-loss
reduction from the first to the last logged epoch falls between 21.7\% and
28.9\%, against 69.5\% to 97.9\% for every other run --- a gap with no runs
inside it, so any threshold placed between the two bands selects the same six.
The rule uses training loss only, never a test score.

The resulting predictors are degenerate in a task-specific way: on NER the
tagger proposes no entity at all, giving precision, recall and F1 of exactly
zero; on text classification the classifier stops predicting a class holding
19\% of the test set. Development scores track test scores in each case, so
the failure is visible without touching the test set. The runs are not
checkpoint mis-selection --- the evaluation-loss minimum across all ten saved
checkpoints is 0.77--0.80 in the failed runs against 0.42--0.55 in the
successful ones --- nor overfitting, since training loss stays high.

The six occur under three different systems, so the instability belongs to the
fine-tuning setup rather than to any initializer. We exclude them from the
means in Table~\ref{tab:main}, mark the affected cells, and report the count.
A single such run, averaged in, moves a cell mean by up to 13 points and
inflates its standard deviation past 30.

\section{Recommendations}

\paragraph{Report the floor.} An initializer that claims to use structural
information should be compared against one that uses none. That comparison
costs one additional system and bounds every other claim in the table; here
it is the difference between ``not separated from FOCUS'' and ``not separated
from a Gaussian''.

\paragraph{Check resolution before comparing.} Whether the between-system
spread exceeds the between-seed spread is computable from a pilot, before any
method is proposed. Where it does not, as in all six cells here, a five-seed
comparison cannot support a ranking regardless of which system wins.

\paragraph{State the attainable $p$.} At five paired seeds the smallest
two-sided exact Wilcoxon $p$ is 0.062. Reporting $p > 0.05$ as evidence of no
difference, in a design where $p < 0.05$ was impossible, misstates what was
tested.

\paragraph{Release the fitted operator, not only the equation.} $W$ in
Equation~\ref{eq:lgse} is a $768 \times 768$ matrix, 2.3\,MB. Releasing it,
or the code that builds it, removes the largest obstacle to reproducing a
method of this kind. We release ours with a sidecar recording its language,
anchor count, objective and residual --- and we found during this work that a
language-mismatched matrix produces entirely plausible results, silently, for
months.

\paragraph{Disclose degenerate runs.} Neither silent exclusion nor silent
averaging is adequate. Report the count, the rule, and the means both ways.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Initialization for vocabulary extension.} WECHSEL
\citep{minixhofer-etal-2022-wechsel} transfers embeddings through bilingual
dictionaries and static vectors. FOCUS \citep{dobler-de-melo-2023-focus},
our near control, builds each new embedding from overlapping tokens in an
auxiliary FastText space. Both FOCUS and the morpheme-grounded method reduce
to a whole-token vector when their condition --- overlap, or an available
decomposition --- does not hold, which bounds how far apart they can be:
their difference is limited by how often that condition fires.
\citet{csaki-etal-2023-efficiently} combine tokenizer extension with a
data-mixing recipe and evaluate it on Hungarian and Thai. Our results do not
argue against this line of work; they indicate that these benchmarks at these
sample sizes cannot adjudicate between its members.

\paragraph{Morphological resources for Ethio-Semitic languages.} Amharic and
Tigrinya are templatic and heavily affixing, which motivates morpheme-aware
vocabulary construction. HornMorpho
\citep{gasser-2011-hornmorpho} provides finite-state analysis and is enabled
in our Amharic configuration; its absence for Tigrinya is the one asymmetry
between our two protocols (\S\ref{sec:setup}).

\paragraph{Reproduction and statistical practice.}
\citet{belz-etal-2021-systematic} survey reproducibility in NLP. Our
methodology is control-based: rather than reporting that numbers differ, we
hold systems that should be unaffected by a change fixed and attribute the
difference from their movement.

\section{Conclusion}

Across thirty cells on a common protocol per language, morpheme-grounded
embedding initialization leads one. Off-the-shelf XLM-R leads three at a
twentieth of the compute, and adaptive pretraining with default
initialization leads two. Against random Gaussian initialization the
morpheme-grounded method is not separated on any task in either language.

We do not conclude that these initializers are equivalent; our tests are too
small to support that and we say so. We conclude that the morpheme-grounded
method as specified does not deliver a measurable advantage on these
benchmarks, that the benchmarks themselves cannot resolve differences of the
size under discussion at the sample sizes conventionally used, and that the
floor comparison against random initialization --- cheap, and omitted from
the published evaluation --- is what makes the first conclusion firm.

\section*{Limitations}

Our $W$ and $\lambda$ are documented substitutions, not values recovered from
the original method, so the \textsc{+LGSE+LAPT} column is one faithful
instantiation and absolute numbers would differ under another. A reader
should treat our comparison as a test of the method as specified, not of
every possible realization of it.

The paired tests have three to ten observations. They establish that these
data do not separate the systems, not that the systems are equivalent;
distinguishing those readings would need many more seeds or benchmarks with a
larger between-system spread. We did not run more seeds: at five, the
attainable $p$ is already above the conventional threshold, so the useful
next step is a different experiment rather than a larger one.

The morpheme-grounded system decomposes 50.0\% of added Amharic tokens and
16.7\% of them from the static lexicon alone, with no analyzer available for
Tigrinya. Its mechanism is therefore exercised unevenly across our two
languages, and more fully where it leads a cell than where it does not. A
version of this study with a Tigrinya analyzer would test the method more
symmetrically.

Amharic text classification uses a corpus we annotated; its label
distribution is skewed and its highest quality level, Excellent, has no test
instances at all. Tigrinya text classification has 74 test items, where one example
moves accuracy by 1.35 points. Neither text-classification cell should carry
much weight.

Two systems' Amharic runs were produced under a configuration whose
alignment-matrix path names the Tigrinya matrix. Neither consumes it, which
Appendix~\ref{app:prov} verifies rather than assumes, but a reader checking
those records will see the path and should know why it is immaterial.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

This paper replaces a retracted paper by the same authors, and withdraws an
intermediate measurement of our own. We report the outcome as measured,
including where it contradicts our earlier claims, and we document errors
made during this work alongside those found in the original: a configuration
error that applied the Tigrinya alignment matrix to Amharic runs, a
morpheme-coverage figure initially over-reported, a hardcoded label set that
inflated one language's text-classification scores, and three log-parsing
defects in our own analysis code. Each is recorded in the repository with the
evidence that found it. We found no evidence of fabrication in the original
work; every discrepancy we traced is consistent with ordinary pipeline
defects of the kind we also made here.

Self-examination carries a conflict of interest. We mitigate it by releasing
every per-run record so that each number can be recomputed independently, by
generating every table from a single committed script, and by reporting the
outcome least favourable to our own prior claim. Readers should weigh our
conclusions accordingly.

All datasets are publicly available or released by us. The Amharic
educational-quality annotation was led by the first author. No human subjects
were involved.

\section{Provenance}
\label{app:prov}

\paragraph{One script.} Every number in this paper is produced by
\texttt{scripts/build\_table2.py}, which reads the per-run
\texttt{experiment.json} records and emits Tables
\ref{tab:main}--\ref{tab:paired}, the per-seed CSV, and the audit below.
Running it reproduces every figure; nothing here was typed by hand.

\paragraph{Runs.} 165 runs across all thirty cells, 0 invalid: 27 cells at
five seeds and the three Amharic NER cells available when the failure mode of
\S\ref{sec:failures} appeared at ten. Each carries its commit, configuration
checksum, alignment-matrix path, corpus checksum, and per-seed development
and test metrics. The audit asserts, per cell, the configuration checksum,
the data directory, and --- for the two systems that read it --- that the
alignment matrix matches the language.

\paragraph{Why two directories supply the Amharic column.} XLM-R, FOCUS and
the morpheme-grounded system come from one directory
(checksum \texttt{3e51cdab\ldots}); \textsc{+LAPT} and
\textsc{+Random+LAPT} from another (\texttt{1fb98f62\ldots}). The two
configurations differ only in the \texttt{alignment\_matrix\_path} line, and
the second names the Tigrinya matrix. That disqualifies the second
directory's FOCUS and morpheme-grounded runs, which is why they are not used.
It does not disqualify \textsc{+LAPT} or \textsc{+Random+LAPT}: neither
consumes FastText, and $W$ is read only on the FastText path. This is
verified, not argued --- all ten of their NER runs are bit-identical to the
corrected-matrix snapshot, difference 0.000000 on every seed, including one
seed recorded under each matrix.

\paragraph{Recipe.} Adaptive pretraining: frozen backbone, maximum length
256, MLM probability 0.15, learning rate $5\times10^{-5}$ constant, batch
size 32, 10 epochs, weight decay 0.01, AdamW, fp16. Fine-tuning: the same
learning rate, schedule, batch size and epoch count, checkpoint selection by
evaluation loss. Identical across all five systems and both languages,
untuned.

\paragraph{Compute.} Each extended-system run takes approximately 5.5 hours
on one A100, dominated by adaptive pretraining, which repeats per seed
because the seed determines the initialization under test. XLM-R runs take
6--24 minutes. The 165 runs total roughly 800 GPU-hours.
