\title{Below the Seed-Noise Floor: A Controlled Comparison of Embedding\\
Initializers for Amharic and Tigrinya Vocabulary Extension}

\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Extending a pretrained encoder's vocabulary for a low-resource language
requires a vector for every token the model has never seen, and a growing
family of methods proposes informed alternatives to random initialization.
We ask whether five such choices can be distinguished at the sample sizes
this literature conventionally uses. Holding one backbone, one adaptation
recipe and one evaluation fixed, we compare off-the-shelf XLM-R against four
vocabulary-extended systems whose new rows are initialized from the
pretrained embedding mean, from a Gaussian, from overlapping tokens in a
FastText space, and from morpheme decompositions, on text classification,
named entity recognition and extractive question answering in Amharic and
Tigrinya. Three findings follow from 165 runs. First, morpheme-grounded
initialization is not separated from the random floor on any task in either
language, and of the 24 comparisons against that floor only two exclude
zero---both favouring simpler informed baselines, by under two points.
Second, the between-system spread is what seed
variation alone produces---a permutation test over system labels reproduces
the observed spread in every cell ($p$ from 0.10 to 0.98), and the variance
attributable to the system is below 5\% throughout---so these designs do not
resolve the comparison they are built to make. Third, a fine-tuning failure
mode affecting 6 of 165 runs is identifiable from training loss alone,
without reference to any test score. We trace the null to a measurable cause:
newly added rows receive only 0.10\%--0.74\% of task tokens, and adaptive
pretraining leaves them at cosine $0.988$ from their initialization, so the
initializations stay distinct while the metric cannot move. All code, per-run records, training
logs and the single script that generates every table are released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A multilingual encoder adapted to a new language usually needs new vocabulary
items, and each new item needs a vector. The choice is not obviously
inconsequential: a randomly drawn row starts orthogonal to everything the
model knows, and several methods therefore initialize new rows from
information the model or an auxiliary space already carries
\citep{minixhofer-etal-2022-wechsel,dobler-de-melo-2023-focus,liu-etal-2024-ofa}.
For morphologically rich languages the argument is sharper still. Amharic and
Tigrinya are templatic and heavily affixing, so a frequency-driven tokenizer
splits a surface form at positions that need not align with any morpheme
boundary, and an initializer that reconstructs a token from its morphemes
could in principle recover structure the tokenizer discards.

Two things are rarely checked when such a method is proposed. The first is a
floor: whether the informed initializer can be separated from a Gaussian
drawn at the pretrained scale, which is the comparison that bounds every
other claim in
the table. The second is resolution: whether the experiment could have
separated the systems at all, given how much the same system moves when only
the random seed changes. A ranking over five systems computed from five seeds
per cell is a ranking over five noisy estimates, and the spread between
system means has a nonzero expectation even when no true difference exists.

This paper runs that check. We fix one backbone, one adaptation recipe and
one evaluation per language, vary only how the new embedding rows are
initialized, and ask what the resulting matrix supports. The answer is that
it supports very little: on these benchmarks, at these sample sizes, the five
systems are not distinguishable, and we can say why.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\itemsep0em
\item A five-system comparison under one protocol per language---3 tasks
  $\times$ 2 languages $\times$ 5 systems, 165 runs---in which 22 of 24
  comparisons against a random floor have intervals containing zero, and the
  two that do not favour simpler baselines by under two points
  (\S\ref{sec:results}, \S\ref{sec:floor}).
\item A resolution analysis that compares each cell's between-system spread
  against the spread expected under no true differences, with a permutation
  test and a variance decomposition, and reports the minimum detectable
  difference per comparison (\S\ref{sec:resolution}).
\item A mechanistic account of the null: new rows receive 0.10\%--0.74\% of
  task tokens, and adaptive pretraining leaves them at cosine $0.988$ from
  their initialization, so the initializations remain distinct while the
  metric cannot respond (\S\ref{sec:coverage}).
\item A fine-tuning failure mode affecting 6 of 165 runs, identified from
  training loss alone and never from a test score, with the exclusion rule
  stated and its effect on each affected cell reported both ways
  (\S\ref{sec:degenerate}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Systems}
\label{sec:systems}

All five systems share one backbone, XLM-R base
\citep{conneau-etal-2020-unsupervised}, and differ only in whether the
vocabulary is extended and how the new rows are initialized.

\paragraph{XLM-R} is the unmodified pretrained encoder. It extends no
vocabulary and performs no adaptive pretraining, so it bounds what the rest
of the machinery is worth.

The remaining four extend the vocabulary with tokens selected by XLM-R's
subword fragmentation rate over the task corpora (198 unique tokens for
Amharic, 198 for Tigrinya), then run language-adaptive pretraining (LAPT) on
monolingual text with the encoder frozen and the embedding matrix trainable.

\paragraph{+Mean+LAPT} keeps whatever the library's resize operation
produces. We state this precisely because it is easy to misread: in
\texttt{transformers} 4.51.3 the default draws new rows from a multivariate
normal with the existing embeddings' mean and covariance, not from a
zero-mean Gaussian. Measured on our setup, the new rows' mean has cosine
similarity $1.0000$ and distance $0.0000$ to the pretrained embedding mean,
against cosine $-0.0345$ for a true random draw at
\texttt{initializer\_range}${=}0.02$. This is therefore a second informed
baseline, not a naive one.

\paragraph{+Random+LAPT} draws each new row from a Gaussian at the pretrained
embedding scale. This is the floor.

\paragraph{+FOCUS+LAPT} initializes each new row as a weighted combination of
the tokens it overlaps with in an auxiliary FastText space
\citep{dobler-de-melo-2023-focus}.

\paragraph{+LGSE+LAPT} is the morpheme-grounded initializer proposed by
\citet{teklehaymanot-etal-2026-lgse}. For a new token $t$ with morpheme
decomposition $m_1 \dots m_k$ it sets
\begin{equation}
e_{\text{new}} = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k} W e_{m_j},
\label{eq:lgse}
\end{equation}
where $e_{m_j}$ is a FastText vector and $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$
maps the FastText space into the model's. Tokens with no decomposition fall
back to character $n$-grams, and during LAPT a regularizer
$\lambda \lVert e_{\text{new}} - \mu \rVert^2$ discourages drift from the
initial vector $\mu$. The originating work reports consistent gains over all
baselines; the present measurements do not reproduce that ordering.

\paragraph{Two quantities we had to choose.}
Equation~\ref{eq:lgse} requires $W$, and the regularizer requires $\lambda$.
Neither is determined by the method as published, so we chose both,
documented them, and held them fixed: $W$ is obtained by orthogonal
Procrustes, $W^\star = \arg\min_{W^\top W = I}\lVert WX - Y \rVert_F$, on
surface-form anchors shared between each language's FastText model and
XLM-R (5{,}910 anchors for Amharic, 3{,}875 for Tigrinya; residual Frobenius
norm 423.0 against 633.0 unaligned, and 315.1 against 490.0), and
$\lambda = 1.0$. These are our choices. The +LGSE+LAPT column is one faithful
instantiation of the method rather than the only one, and a reader should
treat it as such. We note one structural consequence: nothing downstream is a
function of $W$ once initialization is complete, so $W$ receives no gradient
and acts as a fixed preprocessing transform.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{One protocol per language.} Amharic and Tigrinya are kept on
separate protocols and are never pooled. Their class priors and test sizes
differ enough that a cross-language average would not be interpretable: the
Amharic text-classification majority level covers 66\% of test items against
Tigrinya's 32\%, and the Tigrinya test split holds 74 items against Amharic's
235. We report no cross-language statistic and no overall column.

\paragraph{Data.} Amharic NER uses the official MasakhaNER splits
\citep{adelani-etal-2021-masakhaner}; Tigrinya NER uses the MasakhaNER~2.0
Tigrinya splits \citep{adelani-etal-2022-masakhaner2}. Amharic QA uses AmQA
as released \citep{taffa-etal-2024-amqa}. Tigrinya QA is an extractive
SQuAD-format conversion of TIGQA \citep{teklehaymanot-etal-2024-tigqa}: of
2{,}179 raw pairs we keep the 1{,}517 whose answer span matches exactly,
dropping 148 fuzzy alignments and 514 unmatched or unanswerable pairs, split
1189/177/151. Tigrinya text classification uses FineWeb-C
\citep{kydlicek-etal-2025-fineweb2} (\texttt{tir\_Ethi}) at 597/74/74.
Amharic text classification uses a 2{,}499-row annotated educational-quality
corpus; after removing 2 spreadsheet-error rows and 157 exact duplicates,
2{,}340 documents are split 1872/233/235 by the same stratified procedure.
Every split carries a manifest recording source, raw checksum, cleaning
counts and split seed.

\paragraph{The text-classification label set.} Documents carry an
educational-quality level on a five-point ordinal scale, plus a separate flag
for problematic content. We encode the levels as 1--5 and the flag as 6 for
classification, which is our encoding and not an upstream convention. The
flag is not a sixth point above the top level, so our primary metric is
macro-F1 over the quality levels present in the test split, with accuracy
secondary. We report the flag separately: no system on either language at any
seed assigns it, in all fifty text-classification runs, so averaging it into
the primary metric would contribute a guaranteed zero to a sixth of the
score.

\paragraph{Metrics and seeds.} Macro-F1 over the quality scale for text
classification, span F1 for NER, answer F1 for QA. Five seeds (42--46) per
cell, extended to ten (42--51) for three Amharic NER cells. We report mean
$\pm$ sample standard deviation over converged runs.

\paragraph{Seed matching.} A paired comparison uses the seeds present in both
systems after exclusions (\S\ref{sec:degenerate}); a run excluded in either
system removes that seed from the pair. Effective $n$ is therefore reported
per comparison rather than per cell, and it varies from 2 to 9.

\paragraph{One statistical framework.} Intervals are paired-$t$:
$\bar{d} \pm t_{.975,n-1}\, s_d/\sqrt{n}$. Where we also give an exact
Wilcoxon $p$, we report alongside it the smallest $p$ attainable at that $n$,
because the two are easily confused at these sample sizes. We flag the
mixture explicitly rather than leave it implicit.

\paragraph{The morphological configuration differs between the two
languages.} Our Amharic configuration attaches a morphological analyzer as a
segmentation fallback through \texttt{amseg}, whose analyzer is
Amharic-specific; our Tigrinya configuration does not enable one and relies
on the static lexicon alone. This is a property of our configuration and of
the library we used, not a statement about available analyzers: HornMorpho
covers Tigrinya as well \citep{gasser-2011-hornmorpho}. As
\S\ref{sec:coverage} shows, the asymmetry does not disadvantage Tigrinya,
whose lexicon coverage of added tokens is the higher of the two.

\paragraph{Compute.} Each vocabulary-extended run takes about 5.1 hours
(Amharic) or 5.8 hours (Tigrinya) on one A100, dominated by LAPT, which
cannot be shared across seeds because the seed determines the initialization
under test. XLM-R takes 11.1 and 7.2 minutes respectively. The extended
systems therefore cost $27.7\times$ (Amharic) and $47.8\times$ (Tigrinya) the
unmodified baseline at the median, with individual-run ratios spanning
$12\times$ to $94\times$. The 165 runs total roughly 800 GPU-hours.

Table~\ref{tab:main} gives the full matrix. Three observations are worth
making before any comparison is attempted.

\paragraph{No system leads consistently.} Of the four cells we rank,
off-the-shelf XLM-R has the highest mean in two, +LGSE+LAPT in one and
+Mean+LAPT in one; neither text-classification column is ranked. The monotone
ordering such comparisons are often expected to show---uninformed below
informed, informed below morpheme-grounded---appears nowhere.

\paragraph{The unmodified baseline is competitive everywhere.} XLM-R expands
no vocabulary and runs no adaptive pretraining, at between $12\times$ and
$94\times$ less compute, and is never more than 1.7 points behind the best
system in its cell. On both Tigrinya NER and Tigrinya QA it is the best.

\paragraph{Standard deviations are large relative to the differences.}
In Amharic TC the deviations run from 3.24 to 15.56 on a column whose means
span 4.83 points, and in Tigrinya TC from 2.18 to 9.89 on a span of 8.17.
This is why neither column is ranked, and it is the observation
\S\ref{sec:resolution} develops for the table as a whole: at these magnitudes
an ordering is not stable information.

that, nothing else in the table can be read as evidence for informed
initialization. We therefore run it for every system, not only for the one
whose mechanism is under test.

\paragraph{Morpheme-grounded initialization is not separated from the floor
anywhere.} Its six intervals all contain zero, with estimates from $-4.22$ to

proposition the floor comparison was set up to test, since the system that

Tigrinya QA versus XLM-R ($-2.39$, $[-4.56,-0.23]$). The same multiplicity
caution applies, in the same measure.

\section{Resolution}
\label{sec:resolution}

A table of five system means per cell invites a ranking. Whether it supports
one is a separate question, and it has an answer that can be computed before
any system is proposed.

\subsection{The spread expected under no difference}

With $k$ systems, $n$ seeds and no true differences at all, the system means
still differ, because each is an average of $n$ noisy runs. The expected
range of $k$ independent normal means with within-system standard deviation
$\sigma$ is $d_k\,\sigma/\sqrt{n}$, with $d_5 \approx 2.326$. This is the
reference against which an observed spread must be read: a spread of
$1.04\sigma$ with five systems and five seeds is not weak evidence of a
difference, it is the expected consequence of having none.

Table~\ref{tab:resolution} reports the comparison. The observed-to-expected
ratio runs from 0.55 to 1.27 and brackets 1.0: every cell's spread is within
30\% of what pure seed variation produces. The permutation test agrees. In no
cell does shuffling the system labels fail to reproduce the observed spread
routinely---$p$ ranges from 0.101 to 0.976, and the cell with the largest raw
spread (Tigrinya TC, 8.17 points) gives $p = 0.226$.

A variance decomposition says the same thing from a third direction. The
last column of Table~\ref{tab:resolution} gives the share of total variance
attributable to the system: at most 4.2\%, and indistinguishable from zero in
three of six cells, with the balance in seed and residual variation. Whatever
separates these five systems, it is not what dominates the numbers in
Table~\ref{tab:main}.

We state the obvious caveat. A permutation test that fails to reject is not
proof of no difference, and with $n$ between 3 and 5 its power is low; the
same is true of a variance share estimated from five groups. The three
analyses are not independent confirmations so much as three views of one
fact---that within-system variation here is of the same order as
between-system variation---which is why we pair them with the minimum
detectable difference below rather than resting on any one of them.

\subsection{Minimum detectable difference}

The complementary question is what difference these designs could have
detected. For a paired comparison at $\alpha = .05$ two-sided and power
$.80$,
\begin{equation}
\text{MDD} = \left(t_{.975,n-1} + t_{.80,n-1}\right)\frac{s_d}{\sqrt{n}} .
\end{equation}

difference is smaller than the smallest difference the design could reliably
detect. The two exceptions are Amharic QA against +Mean+LAPT and Tigrinya NER
against XLM-R, both unfavourable to +LGSE+LAPT.

The text-classification MDDs deserve emphasis: 18--41 points for Amharic and
13--18 for Tigrinya, against observed differences under 6. No initializer
difference plausible on these tasks is detectable in these cells. That is a
property of the design, not of the methods.

\subsection{What this implies for the sample size}

At five seeds the smallest attainable two-sided exact Wilcoxon $p$ is
$0.0625$, and at four seeds $0.125$. Twenty-two of our 24 comparisons have
$n \leq 5$---only the two Amharic NER comparisons against ten-seed systems
exceed it---so for those 22, $p < 0.05$ is unreachable by construction: no
outcome, however one-sided, could have produced it. (That this is the same
count as the 22 comparisons falling below their MDD is a coincidence; the two
sets are not identical.) A
$p$-value that cannot fall below the threshold it is being compared against
carries no information about the hypothesis, which is why we report intervals
and MDDs throughout and give the attainable floor alongside every $p$ in
Appendix~\ref{app:paired}.

\section{Why the Metric Cannot Move}
\label{sec:coverage}

The resolution analysis shows that these experiments cannot separate the
systems. A different question is why the systems are so close to begin with,
and here two measurements are sufficient.

\paragraph{New rows receive under 1\% of task tokens.} An initializer can
only act through the rows it sets. Tokenizing each test split with XLM-R plus
that language's added tokens, we count how often a token resolves to a newly
added row:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llrrr}
\toprule
Lang. & Task & tokens & new & \% \\
\midrule
am & TC  &  8{,}622 & 39 & 0.45 \\
am & NER & 15{,}872 & 118 & 0.74 \\
am & QA  & 18{,}929 & 19 & 0.10 \\
ti & TC  & 72{,}433 & 110 & 0.15 \\
ti & NER & 30{,}788 & 50 & 0.16 \\
ti & QA  & 12{,}430 & 37 & 0.30 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The maximum is 0.74\%: fewer than one token in 130. Between 3.9\% and 59.5\%
of examples contain at least one such token, but within those examples the
new rows are a small minority of the context. Whatever the initializer does
to these rows, it is acting on under 1\% of the input.

\paragraph{Adaptive pretraining largely preserves the initialization.} A
natural alternative explanation is that LAPT washes out whatever the
initializer wrote. The checkpoints do not support it. Comparing new-token
rows across systems after LAPT (seed 42, Tigrinya NER), the initializations
remain sharply distinct:
+Random+LAPT is essentially orthogonal to every other system (mean cosine
0.001--0.002), +FOCUS+LAPT and +Mean+LAPT sit at 0.760, and +LGSE+LAPT at
0.406 from +Mean+LAPT and 0.452 from +FOCUS+LAPT. Amharic shows the same
pattern with +LGSE+LAPT further from the others (0.087--0.092).

More directly, the morpheme-grounded initialization is deterministic given
$W$ and the lexicon, so we can reconstruct it exactly and compare it against
the trained checkpoint. On Tigrinya NER, seed 42, the mean cosine between a
new row's initial value and its value after LAPT is $0.988$, the median is
$0.99998$, 96.4\% of rows exceed $0.99$, and the mean relative change in
norm is 3.6\%. Consistently, comparing one system across seeds gives mean
cosine $0.9999$ for +Mean+LAPT and $1.0000$ for +LGSE+LAPT, with a maximum
absolute change of about $0.008$; only +Random+LAPT differs across seeds,
because each seed draws a fresh Gaussian. With the encoder frozen and these
rows appearing rarely in the adaptation corpus, LAPT leaves the
initialization substantially where it started.

\paragraph{The two together.} The initializations are distinct, they largely
survive adaptation, and they are what the model uses---yet they touch under
1\% of task tokens. The seed-to-seed variation that dominates
Table~\ref{tab:main} is therefore unlikely to originate in initialization,
and more likely in fine-tuning, which is consistent with +Random+LAPT being
the only system whose new rows differ across seeds while all five systems
show comparable seed variance. We offer this as the most parsimonious account
of the null rather than as a demonstration: the drift measurement covers one
cell and one seed per system, and attributing variance to fine-tuning
directly would require holding the fine-tuning seed fixed while varying the
initialization seed, which our runs do not do.

\paragraph{Coverage of the morpheme path does not explain it.} One might
expect the morpheme-grounded initializer to be inert because few tokens
decompose. For Amharic that is partly so: of 198 added tokens, 45 have a
lexicon entry and 33 (16.7\%) decompose into more than one morpheme, rising
to 99 (50.0\%) with the analyzer attached. For Tigrinya it is not: 197 of 198
added tokens have an entry and 196 (99.0\%) decompose genuinely. The
mechanism is exercised on virtually every added Tigrinya token, and Tigrinya
shows no separation from the floor either (Table~\ref{tab:floor}). Low
decomposition coverage is therefore not what produces the null.

\section{A Fine-Tuning Failure Mode}
\label{sec:degenerate}

Six of the 165 runs never fit their training data. They are identifiable from
the training logs alone, which matters: an exclusion rule that consults test
scores cannot be used to decide what counts as a measurement.

\paragraph{The rule.} For each run we compute the relative reduction in
training loss from the first to the last logged epoch. On Amharic the
distribution is bimodal with an empty interval between the modes: six runs
fall between 21.69\% and 28.91\%, and every other run between 69.45\% and
97.91\%. Any threshold inside that gap selects the same six runs; we use the
midpoint, 49.18\%. The six are Amharic NER (+FOCUS+LAPT seed 44, +LGSE+LAPT
seed 45) and Amharic TC (XLM-R seeds 42 and 45, +LGSE+LAPT seeds 42 and 43).
Four of the six are text-classification runs, and the affected cells are
marked in Table~\ref{tab:main}.

\paragraph{What the excluded runs look like.} The NER failures produce a
tagger that proposes no entity, so precision and recall are both exactly
zero. The TC failures produce a classifier that collapses onto few classes.
Neither is a crash: the runs complete, LAPT loss descends normally
beforehand, and total runtime is unchanged.

\paragraph{Effect of the exclusions.} Including the two Amharic NER zeros
changes +Random+LAPT from $67.78 \pm 3.25$ to $54.22 \pm 30.44$ and
+FOCUS+LAPT from $66.78 \pm 2.93$ to $60.10 \pm 22.33$. A single zero moves a
five-seed mean by 13.6 points and inflates its standard deviation almost
tenfold, so a deviation of that size is a collapse indicator rather than a
measure of seed variance. We report both forms rather than choose silently.

\paragraph{Tigrinya text classification behaves differently, and we do not
apply the rule there.} In that cell the training loss is flat for every
system and every seed: relative reduction runs from 2.9\% to 8.8\%, with
losses oscillating (for instance $1.4529 \rightarrow 1.3955$ over ten
epochs). There is no bimodal structure and no gap, so the Amharic threshold
would mark all fifteen runs. Absolute loss reduction is not comparable across
corpora of different sizes---597 Tigrinya training documents against 1{,}872
Amharic---and we therefore exclude nothing in that cell and decline to rank
it. Consistent with the flat loss, 7 of 25 Tigrinya TC runs predict a single
class on the test split, against 2 of 25 for Amharic. We report this as a
property of the cell rather than as an exclusion criterion, since it is
measured on test output.

\section{Recommendations}
\label{sec:recs}

Each of these is cheap, and each would have changed what our own tables could
support.

\paragraph{Report the floor.} An informed initializer should be compared
against a Gaussian at the pretrained scale, in the same table, on the same
seeds. It is one extra column and it bounds every other claim. Without it a
reader cannot tell whether a method is separated from its competitors or
merely participates in the same noise.

\paragraph{Check resolution from a pilot, before proposing a method.}
Run one system at several seeds, estimate $\sigma$, and compare
$d_k\sigma/\sqrt{n}$ against the effect you expect to claim. If the expected
spread under no difference is comparable to your anticipated effect, the
experiment cannot support a ranking regardless of the outcome. This costs a
handful of runs and is computable before the method exists.

\paragraph{State the attainable $p$ at your sample size.} At five paired
observations the smallest exact two-sided Wilcoxon $p$ is 0.0625. Reporting
$p > 0.05$ at $n = 5$ as a negative finding, or hoping for $p < 0.05$,
mistakes an arithmetic ceiling for an empirical result.

\paragraph{Release the fitted operator, not only the equation.} A method that
requires a fitted transform is not reproducible from its equation alone. Ours
is a $768 \times 768$ matrix, 2.3\,MB; we release it with a sidecar recording
its language, anchor count, objective and residual norm, so a reader can tell
at a glance whether they hold the right one.

\paragraph{Disclose degenerate runs with the rule and the count.} Averaging a
zero into a five-seed mean misstates a baseline by over 13 points; dropping
it silently is equally misleading. State a rule computed from training
behaviour, report the count, and give the affected means both ways.

\paragraph{Name the resize semantics.} A library default is part of the
experimental condition. In current \texttt{transformers}, resizing an
embedding matrix initializes new rows from the existing mean and covariance,
so a column labelled "default initialization" is an informed baseline. A
reader who assumes a zero-mean Gaussian misreads the whole table.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Embedding initialization under vocabulary extension.}
WECHSEL \citep{minixhofer-etal-2022-wechsel} transfers embeddings through
bilingual dictionaries and static word vectors. FOCUS
\citep{dobler-de-melo-2023-focus} initializes each new token as a sparse
weighted combination of overlapping tokens in an auxiliary FastText space;
OFA \citep{liu-etal-2024-ofa} factorizes the embedding matrix to initialize
many languages at once. The morpheme-grounded initializer we evaluate belongs
to this family and reduces, like FOCUS, to a whole-token vector when its
decomposition is unavailable. \citet{csaki-etal-2023-efficiently} study
tokenizer extension and data mixing for adaptation. Our results do not argue
against this line of work; they indicate that the benchmarks and sample sizes
used to compare its members do not resolve the comparison, which is a
statement about evaluation practice rather than about any method.

\paragraph{Adaptation for Ethio-Semitic languages.} Both languages are
templatic and heavily affixing, which is what motivates morpheme-aware
vocabulary construction. HornMorpho
\citep{gasser-2011-hornmorpho} provides finite-state analysis for Amharic,
Oromo and Tigrinya. Multilingual encoders covering these languages include
AfroXLMR \citep{alabi-etal-2022-adapting} and Glot500
\citep{imanigooghari-etal-2023-glot500}, and EthioLLM
\citep{tonja-etal-2024-ethiollm} targets Ethiopian languages directly. Our
task resources come from MasakhaNER
\citep{adelani-etal-2021-masakhaner,adelani-etal-2022-masakhaner2}, AmQA
\citep{taffa-etal-2024-amqa}, TIGQA
\citep{teklehaymanot-etal-2024-tigqa} and FineWeb-C
\citep{kydlicek-etal-2025-fineweb2}.

\paragraph{Seed variance and reproducibility.}
\citet{dodge-etal-2020-fine} show that fine-tuning outcomes vary substantially
with the random seed and that reporting practice often understates it;
\citet{reimers-gurevych-2017-reporting} make the same argument for sequence
labelling, and \citet{gorman-bedrick-2019-need} show that conclusions can
reverse under a different split. \citet{belz-etal-2021-systematic} survey
reproducibility research in NLP and advocate holding unaffected components
fixed to attribute differences. Our resolution analysis applies that
reasoning quantitatively: rather than reporting that systems differ, we ask
whether the design could have detected a difference, and answer it with a
null reference, a permutation test and a variance decomposition.

\section{Conclusion}

On three tasks in two languages, at five to ten seeds per cell, five
embedding initialization strategies are not distinguishable. No initializer
separates from a Gaussian floor on any task; the between-system spread is
within 30\% of what seed variation alone produces and is reproduced by
shuffling system labels in every cell; the system accounts for under 5\% of
variance throughout; and 22 of 24 paired differences fall below the smallest
difference the design could detect.

We can also say why the systems are close. Newly added embedding rows receive
between 0.10\% and 0.74\% of task tokens, and adaptive pretraining leaves
those rows nearly where the initializer put them---the initializations remain
distinct after adaptation, and nearly identical across seeds. The rows the
initializer controls are simply not exercised often enough for the metric to
respond, while the seed-to-seed variation of fine-tuning is.

We do not conclude that informed initialization is without value. We conclude
that on these benchmarks, at these sample sizes, the question is not
resolvable, and that the checks which would have revealed this---a floor
column, a resolution estimate from a pilot, and a stated minimum detectable
difference---are available before any method is proposed.

\section*{Limitations}

Our $W$ and $\lambda$ are documented choices rather than values recovered
from the originating method, which specifies neither, so the +LGSE+LAPT
column is one faithful instantiation and absolute comparisons to published
figures are not meaningful. The paired comparisons have two to nine
observations each and are correspondingly underpowered: they establish that
these data do not separate the systems, not that the systems are equivalent,
and distinguishing those two readings would require either many more seeds or
benchmarks whose between-system spread exceeds their seed noise.

The morphological mechanism is exercised unevenly across the two languages
(99.0\% of added Tigrinya tokens decompose against 16.7\% of Amharic tokens
under the static lexicon, or 50.0\% with the analyzer our Amharic
configuration attaches), so the two columns do not test the same thing to the
same degree. The text-classification cells are the weakest: Amharic's test
split contains no instances of the top quality level, because the corpus
holds only two and the split guard retains both in training, and Tigrinya's
74 test items move accuracy by 1.35 points each. Tigrinya text classification
is additionally reported without a ranking (\S\ref{sec:degenerate}). Our
coverage measurement counts how often a new row is used, not how much it
matters when used; a token can be rare and still decisive. Finally, we study
one backbone and one adaptation recipe at one scale, and a larger model, a
longer adaptation corpus or an unfrozen encoder could change how much the
initialization survives.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

The authors evaluate an initialization method they have previously proposed.
To mitigate the conflict of interest this creates, every per-run record,
training log and data manifest is released, and every number reported here is
generated by a single committed script from those records rather than
transcribed, so any figure can be recomputed independently.

All datasets are publicly available or released with this work. No human
subjects were involved, and no personal data was collected.

\paragraph{One script produces every table.} Running
\texttt{scripts/build\_table2.py --out tables} reads the 165 per-run records
and regenerates Tables~\ref{tab:main}--\ref{tab:mdd} and the per-seed CSV. It

corpus checksum, FastText manifest, elapsed time, and per-seed development
and test metrics including per-class support and F1. The released per-seed
CSV carries one row per (language, task, system, seed) with the primary
score, the secondary metrics, the exclusion flag, and the source record's
path.

\paragraph{Configuration groups.} The Amharic runs carry two configuration
checksums and the Tigrinya runs one. The two Amharic configurations differ
only in the \texttt{alignment\_matrix\_path} line: the group containing
+Mean+LAPT and +Random+LAPT names the other language's matrix. Neither of
those two systems reads it. $W$ is loaded only on the FastText-consuming
path, which +FOCUS+LAPT and +LGSE+LAPT take and the other three do not. This
is verified rather than argued: all ten of those two systems' Amharic NER
runs are bit-identical across the two matrix settings, difference
$0.000000$ on every seed, including one seed recorded under each. We state
this here because a reader inspecting the records will see the path and needs
the fact.

\paragraph{Unavailable artifacts.} FastText models are about 3\,GB each and
are not included; \texttt{data/fasttext\_manifest.json} records the training
command, dimension, vocabulary size and checksum for both, and the pipeline
fetches or rebuilds them on first use. The alignment matrices are released
with sidecars recording language, anchor count, objective and residual
Frobenius norm.
